# OMARO Ontology reference

## Scope

OMARO—the **Ontology for Multiperspectivity, Assertions, and Review in
Organology**—is a formal OWL vocabulary and an operational validation model for
contextualized organological claims. It represents who made a claim, from
which perspective, about which target and scheme concept, under what
applicability conditions, with what evidence and authority, and with which
independent review decisions.

OMARO is not a complete ontology of instrument materials, parts, acoustics,
performance, or social function. Those domains can be linked through governed
external identifiers or future modules. Hornbostel–Sachs and the MIMO
instrument thesaurus remain independent SKOS concept schemes used by the
reference dataset; they are not redefined as OMARO classes.

The normative compact ontology is generated locally in Turtle, JSON-LD, and RDF/XML at
`dist/ontology/0.1.0/`. It contains ontology metadata and the owned class and
property vocabulary, but no reference-dataset instances. The combined project
graph remains in `dist/rdf/`. JSON Schemas define closed record shapes, SHACL
validates the dataset projection, and `DATA_DICTIONARY.md` defines field-level
behavior. This reference explains how the pieces fit together.

The compact ontology also publishes the four OMARO mapping-purpose SKOS
concepts and their concept scheme because `mappingPurpose` records depend on
those stable terms. They are ontology-owned code resources, not empirical
mapping assertions or reference-dataset content.

| Term namespace | `https://w3id.org/modavis/omaro#` |
| Prefix | `omaro` |
| License | CC0 1.0 |

OMARO reuses RDF, RDFS, OWL, SKOS, SKOS-XL, PROV-O, SOSA/SSN, Dublin Core,
DCAT, VANN, and Schema.org terms, with selective CIDOC CRM, CRMsci, and QUDT
alignments. It records conservative discovery relations to VAO 0.4.0 and
MODAVIS 0.1.0. It does not declare broad `owl:imports`, equivalence, or identity
relations to those standards. OntoLex is intentionally deferred because the
current source-qualified labels do not establish lexical entries, grammatical
forms, or senses. A CRMinf projection is also deferred: naming an inference
logic does not supply the premise and conclusion beliefs and proposition sets
required for a faithful CRMinf inference-making graph.

DCAT 3, DQV, and RO-Crate are release-level profiles rather than additions to
the OMARO domain vocabulary. DCAT describes the maintained and versioned
datasets and their distributions; DQV records defined dataset-quality
measurements; RO-Crate makes an archive self-describing. None changes an
OMARO assertion's stance, review status, applicability, authority, cultural
validity, or publication authorization.

## Conceptual pattern

An OMARO classification is a proposition entity generated by a documented
assignment activity, not an unqualified target/class pair:

```text
target state -> observation assessment -> actual observation/result when made
             -> criterion/inference
             -> classification assignment activity
             -> classification assertion entity
```

The assignment uses `omaro:usedAssessment` for every declared assessment
premise. `omaro:usedObservation` is emitted only when that premise records an
actual `detected`, `not-detected`, or `indeterminate` attempt. This distinction
prevents the range of `usedObservation` from reclassifying `not-observed` or
`not-applicable` assessments as observation activities.

Review activities generate separate decision entities. A rejection in one
validation dimension does not erase an acceptance in another. Projection
policies decide which qualified occurrences are exposed in a source-faithful,
research, or direct endorsed view.

All owned classes are declared `owl:Class` and `rdfs:Class`, with English label,
definition, and `rdfs:isDefinedBy` links. OMARO deliberately omits broad OWL
domain axioms for reused properties: one property can be used across several
assertion classes, and multiple `rdfs:domain` declarations would otherwise
imply unintended intersections.

The four assertion classes are subclasses of `rdf:Statement` and `prov:Entity`,
matching their explicit subject/predicate/object projection.
`omaro:ClassificationAssignment` and `omaro:ReviewEvent` are `prov:Activity`;
the former also aligns with CIDOC CRM `E17 Type Assignment`.
`omaro:OrganologicalObservation` is a subclass of
`omaro:ObservationAssessment` and aligns with SOSA `Observation` and CRMsci
[`S27 Observation`](https://cidoc-crm.org/extensions/crmsci/html/CRMsci_v3.2.html).
Only rows whose status is `detected`, `not-detected`, or `indeterminate` receive
those types. `not-observed` and `not-applicable` rows remain
`omaro:ObservationAssessment` instances because no observation activity is
asserted. The narrower CRMsci `S4 Single Observation` is not used: it requires
one binary observed proposition. A classification criterion is a `prov:Plan`.
Other durable record-like resources are generally `prov:Entity` subclasses.
These alignments support provenance and museum-science queries without turning
independent Hornbostel–Sachs concepts into OMARO classes.

The entity/activity distinction is also explicit in ten local
`owl:disjointWith` axioms: each assignment/review activity class is disjoint
with the four assertion classes and `ReviewDecision`. Cross-registry URI
collisions fail canonical validation, and SHACL rejects collapsed RDF nodes.
The rationale, exact boundary, and tested entailment regime are documented in
[INFERENCE_AND_VALIDATION.md](INFERENCE_AND_VALIDATION.md). These constraints
do not assert that every organological target kind is mutually exclusive.

## Properties

Every owned property is declared either `owl:ObjectProperty` or
`owl:DatatypeProperty`. URI-valued properties are object properties; literal
codes, text, dates, counts, and Boolean controls are datatype properties.

`ConceptRelationAssertion` requires one or more `mappingPurpose` links for
SKOS mapping predicates and none for structural `broader`, `narrower`, or
`related` claims. Mapping assertions are not flattened into bare SKOS mapping
triples because that would erase their stated purpose. A purpose records
operational suitability only: `ProtocolApplication`, `UseDecision`, ODRL, and
applicable law or community protocol remain the separate authorization layer.

These resources do not equate epistemic acceptance, legal rights, collective
authorization, individual consent, and permission for a publication action.
The public build evaluates the latter independently and fails closed for
community-governed material. A verified protocol uses either a locally
recomputed digest over a safe dataset-relative artifact or an identified
external attestation; `machine-enforceable` is stricter and permits only the
local method. A publication grant must cover all requested targets and scopes,
all applicable protocols, and any restricted purpose or audience. Its deciding
agent's active mandate must name the exact requested action. Both `documented`
and `not-required` legal-basis or consent states require a public-safe reference
and accountable assessor.

## Obvious Hornbostel–Sachs multiplicity examples

### Double bass: technique changes the applicable suffix

MIMO instrument concept `InstrumentsKeywords/3111` maps to the morphological
class `321.322`, the finger-plucked form `321.322-5`, and the bowed form
`321.322-71`. One physical double bass can participate in both pizzicato and
arco realizations. The technique-specific assignments therefore belong on
separate `sounding-realization` targets, or on a physical-object target
with separate `specified` scopes using `appliesToPlayingTechnique`.

```turtle
@prefix omaro: <https://w3id.org/modavis/omaro#> .
@prefix ex: <https://example.org/omaro-evaluation/> .
@prefix hs: <http://www.mimo-db.eu/HornbostelAndSachs/> .
@prefix rdf: <http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#> .
@prefix prov: <http://www.w3.org/ns/prov#> .

ex:pizzicato-scope a omaro:ApplicabilityScope ;
    omaro:scopeMode omaro:scope-mode-specified ;
    omaro:appliesToPlayingTechnique ex:pizzicato .

ex:pizzicato-assignment a omaro:ClassificationAssignment ;
    omaro:assignedBy ex:cataloguer ;
    omaro:generatedAssertion ex:pizzicato-claim .

ex:pizzicato-claim a omaro:ClassificationAssertion ;
    rdf:subject ex:double-bass-42-pizzicato ;
    rdf:predicate omaro:classifiedAs ;
    rdf:object hs:6471 ;
    prov:wasGeneratedBy ex:pizzicato-assignment ;
    omaro:targetType omaro:target-type-sounding-realization ;
    omaro:perspective ex:cataloguing-perspective ;
    omaro:hasApplicabilityScope ex:pizzicato-scope .
```

The fragment is explanatory: a conforming canonical record must also provide
the required scheme, agent, method, evidence, lifecycle, policy, source, and
authority fields.

### Alto trombone: construction changes the applicable class

MIMO concept `InstrumentsKeywords/4361` maps to slide class `423.22`
(`HornbostelAndSachs/356`) and valve class `423.233.1`
(`HornbostelAndSachs/2160`). A catalogue must not assign both detailed classes
to every object called an alto trombone. It should inspect the object and make
one physical-object assertion with a `specified` configuration scope, or
identify separate `instrument-configuration` targets for a hybrid or
reconfigurable object.

These examples are multiplicity inside one MIMO source perspective. They show
why OMARO needs perspective and scope, but they do not by themselves establish
multi-community agreement or cultural validity.

## Cultural validity

OMARO can represent an authorized community review, but it cannot manufacture
authority or consent. A cultural-validity claim requires a named community
perspective, a documented `AuthorityAssignment`, evidence, a community-scoped
`ReviewDecision` generated by a `ReviewEvent`, and a policy that explicitly
recognizes that decision. Scholarly, linguistic, ethical, and rights decisions
remain separate. Conflicting active reviews can coexist and be queried by
scope, perspective, time, and policy. A separate `ProtocolApplication` and
active `UseDecision` may still be required before publication.

## Conformance and safe use

Canonical validation checks the complete declared assessment chain. A
referenced criterion must agree with the assertion's scheme, stated scheme
version, conclusion class, applicability scopes, and inference logic. Each
referenced assessment must match the observable-property and procedure
restrictions of at least one criterion, and each restricting criterion must
have a matching premise when premises are supplied. The assessed feature or
part must be the assertion target, a target connected through the registered
configuration/component/condition/performance graph, or a target that realizes
the asserted instrument concept. These constraints reject incoherent links;
they do not derive a classification or certify its truth.

A conforming consumer should:

1. use URI identity rather than labels or row positions;
2. preserve assertion entities, assignment activities, and their provenance;
3. distinguish perspective from applicability scope;
4. treat unresolved scope as unknown, never universal;
5. retain review decisions independently rather than collapsing them to one status;
6. apply the named projection policy before emitting a direct assertion;
7. evaluate action-specific publication authorization independently; and
8. never present automated findings as human, scholarly, or cultural validation.
