Two different claims can coexist. You can examine the disagreement without erasing either source.

Why not just give each instrument one category?

A category can be useful, but an unqualified link loses who made the assignment and when it applies. OMARO preserves separate occurrences, even when their target and classification are identical. It can then produce a simpler view only when an explicit review policy permits it.

Hornbostel–Sachs categories remain vocabulary concepts. Their hierarchy does not automatically become a set of OWL classes of physical instruments.

02 Understand targets, claims and evidence

Describe the right thing

A concept, physical object, component, functional module, aggregate, configuration, condition, sounding realization, performance event and ensemble medium are distinct modeling targets. A bagpipe’s chanter and whole instrument need separate targets; a part’s class is not automatically the whole’s class.

A statement and the act of making it have different identities

A classification assertion is the proposition. Its assignment is an activity. A review event and its resulting decision likewise remain separate. Two occurrences with the same target and class are still separately attributable.

An assessment does not always mean an observation occurred

Five assessment statuses
StatusInterpretation
detectedAn actual attempt with a positive or categorical result.
not-detectedAn actual attempt with an explicit negative result.
indeterminateAn attempt occurred, but its outcome is unresolved.
not-observedNo observation was made.
not-applicableThe assessment is inapplicable.

Every assessment premise uses usedAssessment; only actual attempts also use usedObservation. Procedures, properties, units and directional evidence remain explicit.

Compound classifications preserve token order and uncertain suffixes. They stay tokenized-uninterpreted until documented interpretation supplies structure. An expert interpretation is not review approval.

03 Explore scope, disagreement and review

Scope can name community, place, period, usage domain, technique, configuration, language variety and a temporal interval. Within a dimension, values are alternatives. Across dimensions, conditions must hold together. Several scopes are alternative complete combinations.

Seven ways to state applicability
Scope modeWhat it says
source-silentThe source supplied no scope; applicability is unknown.
not-yet-investigatedScope has not been researched.
known-unknownInvestigation could not resolve it.
intentionally-unscopedThe contributor deliberately leaves it open.
not-applicableContext matching is not meaningful for this assertion type.
specifiedExplicit contextual boundaries are given.
context-independentThe modeled dimensions do not change this identified claim; this is not universal cultural truth.

at asks about subject-matter time. as_of asks which claims and review decisions were active. Superseded decisions do not revive when their successors expire; suspension is a separate lifecycle.

A direct endorsed classification requires policy opt-in, eligible stance, active lifecycle, context-independent claim and supporting-review scopes, the required independent authorized acceptances, and no active authorized veto. A narrower or unresolved veto blocks a static projection. In a contextual query, it is ignored only when supplied context proves disjointness.

04 Preserve names, vocabularies and mapping purposes

Instrument names and Hornbostel–Sachs categories remain SKOS concepts. Their hierarchy does not entail an OWL class hierarchy or an intersection of physical instruments.

SKOS-XL labels are assertion-scoped occurrences: identical text does not establish identity. Submitted language tags remain distinct from canonical BCP 47 interpretations. Script detection cannot establish a language, dialect or community preference.

A qualified mapping states its purpose, such as query expansion or scholarly comparison. It stays qualified instead of becoming an unrestricted mapping triple. Structural broader, narrower and related assertions carry no mapping-purpose list. Operational suitability never grants permission for the proposed use.

05 Keep review and permission independent

Scholarly acceptance, legal rights, collective authorization, individual consent and publication permission answer different questions. A positive answer to one does not establish the others.

Use permission must match the exact action, targets, communities, purpose, audience, scope, time and applicable protocols. A display grant cannot authorize bulk export or indexing. Only a matching, verified local protocol artifact can support machine enforcement; an external attestation alone cannot.

Publication authorization runs before generating outputs or search results. Missing, withheld, expired or mismatched permissions fail closed. No interface option in this guide grants consent or authorizes real governed data.

The illustrative records describe no real community and provide no community approval. Accountable human and community processes establish those decisions.

07 Follow the formal model and scientific sources

Inference

OWL describes semantic consequences with open-world assumptions. Missing information is not automatically false.

Validation

JSON Schema, the canonical model and SHACL check structure, references and cross-record constraints.

Policy

Review policies select qualified claims. They do not execute as OWL theorems or establish empirical truth.

Ten local OWL disjointness axioms make the activity/entity boundary explicit, following PROV’s entity/activity distinction. No unique-name assumption or automatic identity from equal labels is introduced.

  • W3C, OWL 2 Primer — inference and open-world semantics.
  • W3C, PROV-CONSTRAINTS, constraint 55 — entity/activity separation.
  • W3C, SHACL — graph validation.
  • W3C, RDF 1.1 Semantics, reification — describing a statement does not assert its triple.
  • Kolozali, Barthet, Fazekas & Sandler (2011), Knowledge Representation Issues in Musical Instrument Ontology Design, ISMIR, pp. 465–470 — classification and domain modeling.
  • Lysloff & Matson (1985), A New Approach to the Classification of Sound-Producing Instruments — method-dependent, multidimensional comparison.
