# Decolonial commitments and operational limits

## Position

This project is decolonization-aware and structurally capable of representing
community-governed perspectives. It is not described as decolonized. A schema
cannot create consent, representative legitimacy, institutional power sharing,
or community benefit.

The source-faithful MIMO graph is preserved as historical/institutional
evidence. Decolonial work applies to authority, contextualization, project
defaults, rights, participation, and downstream use; it does not rewrite a
source witness or replace it with a supposedly universal alternative.

This position follows the distinction between a knowledge-organization system
and a formal domain ontology in the [W3C SKOS Reference](https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/),
the provenance specialization approach of [PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/),
the Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, and Ethics focus
of the [CARE Principles](https://www.gida-global.org/s/dsj-1158_carroll.pdf),
and the community-controlled protocol and permission model described by
[Local Contexts](https://localcontexts.org/labels/traditional-knowledge-labels/).
Where Indigenous peoples and knowledge are implicated, the project must also
respect the rights articulated in the
[United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples](https://legal.un.org/avl/ha/ga_61-295/ga_61-295.html).

## Already enforced

- Hornbostel–Sachs/MIMO claims are attributable occurrences, not universal
  facts.
- Source silence is explicit and cannot become context-independent validity.
- Community validity requires exact mandate coverage for every represented
  community.
- Scholarly or technical competence is not substituted for community
  authority.
- Active disagreement remains visible and veto rules prevent a simplified
  endorsement from concealing it.
- Rights/protocol evidence and retroactive versus prospective revocation are
  distinguishable.
- Source-layer CC0 does not automatically license future contributed material.
- Local Contexts Labels or similar community-controlled instruments may be
  recorded only when supplied or authorized through their proper governance
  process; repository personnel do not assign them on a community's behalf.

## Not yet realized

The canonical dataset currently has no community authority assignment,
community review, community scheme, or community-contributed label. It has not
yet demonstrated shared governance, paid participation, benefit sharing,
record-level consent enforcement, access restrictions, or community-controlled
release decisions.

## Operational gates before public claims

The project may say that it incorporates decolonial commitments only after a
documented pilot demonstrates all of the following:

1. community-led participation in model and interface design, not only review;
2. a verifiable mandate and community-defined decision procedure;
3. budgeted compensation and documented benefit sharing;
4. record-level consent, display, indexing, reuse, export, withdrawal, and
   access controls enforced by applications;
5. no rights leakage from source-layer CC0 to contributions;
6. community control over names, protocols, and project-facing presentation;
7. representation of dissent or internal plurality without forcing consensus;
8. public reporting of decision rights, compensation, unresolved disputes, and
   outcomes; and
9. evaluation by the participating community of whether the work was useful
   and beneficial.

The stronger adjective “decolonized” is not an appropriate release claim.

## Modules that require co-design

### Rights and consent

Accuracy review, community acceptance, consent to collect, permission to
display, permission to index, permission to reuse, and access restrictions are
not interchangeable. A future rights-and-consent module must make these
decisions independently enforceable and must support withdrawal without
erasing the historical audit trail.

### Contributed lexical knowledge

Community, performer, maker, scholarly, and curatorial names need origin,
language variety, community, evidence, pronunciation/media, term role, rights,
protocol, scope, review, withdrawal, supersession, display, and search
semantics. Those assertions must not be forced into an unqualified preferred
SKOS label merely to become visible.

### Colonial and custody provenance

Physical-object modeling must cover makers and originating communities,
collecting/removal/acquisition events, custodians, coercion or uncertainty,
missionary/military/scientific/commercial contexts, restitution or repatriation
requests, digital surrogates, and media restrictions. These facts belong to a
physical-object/provenance module, not a classification scope note.

### Autonomous schemes

Community or regional taxonomies must be independent, governed concept schemes
with their own relations, language, definitions, versions, and rights.
Crosswalks to Hornbostel–Sachs remain qualified, purpose-specific assertions;
they do not reduce a community concept to a local equivalent of an HS class.
